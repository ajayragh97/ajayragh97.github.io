# The Thing in the Woods

Julian had stayed behind at the picnic tables, refusing to give up his lunch break for what he called "a walk through bug-infested dirt." So, it was just Lukas, Devan, and Clara pushing their way through the dense brush behind the school.

The autumn air was usually cool and crisp, but as they walked deeper into the woods, it began to feel oddly heavy. 

"Keep an eye out for poison oak," Clara warned. She was pushing branches aside with a long stick. "Three leaves, let it be. I'm serious, Devan."

"Relax, I'm watching," Devan said from the front. He moved through the trees without making a single sound. While Lukas and Clara crunched loudly over dry leaves and snapped twigs, Devan's feet seemed to glide over the forest floor. "Whatever hit the ground sounded heavy. We should be close."

Lukas didn't say anything. He couldn't. 

He was too busy focusing on the bizarre feeling in his chest. The dull ache from earlier was gone. Now, it felt like an invisible string was tied around his ribs, gently pulling him forward. With every step they took, the exhausted, heavy feeling in his muscles started to fade. It was replaced by a fresh, cooling rush, like drinking ice water on a hot day. 

*What is wrong with me?* he thought, rubbing his chest. *Why do I feel better the further we go?*

"Do you smell that?" Clara asked suddenly, stopping in her tracks. She lifted her nose to the air. 

Lukas sniffed. It smelled like burning ozone—like the sharp, metallic scent left in the air right after a huge lightning strike. 

"Over here," Devan called out quietly. He had stopped moving and was crouched low behind a thick oak tree, looking down into a small clearing. 

Lukas and Clara jogged over and knelt beside him. 

Lukas peeked around the rough bark of the tree, and his breath caught in his throat. 

In the middle of the clearing, there was a fresh crater in the dirt. It wasn't huge—maybe the size of a kiddie pool—but the earth had been tossed aside violently. The strange thing was that there were no fallen trees or broken branches above it. Whatever made the hole hadn't crashed down through the leaves. It was as if it had simply popped into existence right on the ground. 

"Look at the grass," Clara whispered, leaning forward with wide eyes. 

She pointed to the edges of the dirt hole. The weeds and grass touching the crater weren't just green—they were a strange, glowing purple color. And even crazier, they seemed to be twisting and growing right in front of their eyes, curling over the dirt like tiny snakes. 

"That's not normal," Clara said, her voice shaking slightly. "Plants don't do that. Not on this planet."

But Lukas barely heard her. His eyes were locked on the center of the crater. 

Sitting in the loose dirt was a chunk of dark, jagged stone, about the size of a football. But it wasn't just a rock. Deep glowing blue veins ran through it, pulsing slowly like a heartbeat. A faint, icy mist rolled off it, hugging the ground. 

As soon as Lukas laid eyes on the rock, the hum in his chest turned into a roar. His fingers began to tingle. The blue light from the rock felt like a magnet, dragging him forward. Before he even realized what he was doing, he stood up and stepped into the clearing. 

"Lukas, what are you doing?" Devan hissed, reaching out to grab him. "Don't go out there!"

Lukas ignored him. His body was starving for whatever that rock was giving off. He walked down into the shallow dirt hole and stopped right in front of the stone. 

The closer he got, the brighter the blue light glowed. He reached his hand out. 

"Lukas, stop! It might be radioactive!" Clara yelled, finally abandoning her hiding spot and running after him. 

Before she could reach him, Lukas's fingertips brushed the surface of the strange stone. 

It didn't burn. Instead, a massive rush of pure energy blasted up his arm. It felt exactly like the moment his computer at home finally clicked into a fast Wi-Fi connection, only a thousand times stronger. A soft blue glow flashed under the skin of his hand, mirroring the veins in the rock. 

The exhaustion. The pain. The brain fog. All of it vanished instantly. Lukas felt powerful, wide awake, and incredibly light. He felt like he could run ten miles without breaking a sweat. 

But as the energy rushed into him, the rock began to change. 

The bright blue veins inside the stone flickered, then dimmed. In less than three seconds, the glow completely died out. The icy mist vanished. The stone turned into a dull, ugly piece of grey rock. 

Lukas pulled his hand back, gasping. He stumbled backward and fell hard on his hands and knees, staring at his palms. They looked perfectly normal. 

Clara rushed down beside him, grabbing his arm. "Are you okay?! Did it burn you?" 

"I'm... I'm fine," Lukas breathed out, his eyes wide. He wasn't just fine. He felt amazing. "It didn't hurt. It just... turned off."

Devan slid into the crater next to them, poking the now-dead rock with his shoe. "Turned off? Dude, it looked like you just absorbed its battery."

"I don't know what happened," Lukas lied. Well, it was a half-lie. He didn't know *how* it happened, but he knew exactly what his body had done. It had eaten the energy from the rock. 

*Snap.* 

The sharp sound of a heavy branch breaking echoed from the other side of the woods. 

All three of them froze. 

Devan instantly stood up, putting himself between Lukas and the trees. He lowered his center of gravity, falling right into one of his martial arts stances. 

A mechanical hum filled the air. It sounded like a giant hornet. 

Hovering through the trees, about ten feet off the ground, was a drone. But this wasn't a cheap toy with a camera. It was sleek, completely silent except for the low hum, and painted pitch black. A red scanning laser shot down from its belly, sweeping across the forest floor. 

It was scanning for the rock. 

"Hide," Devan ordered in a low, tight voice. 

He shoved Lukas and Clara back down into the dirt hole, throwing himself flat on top of them just as the red laser swept over their heads. They pressed their faces into the dirt, not daring to breathe. 

"Scanning anomaly zero-four," a flat, robotic voice crackled from a small speaker on the drone. "Energy signature has dropped to zero. Proceeding with ground team check."

Through the trees, far away but getting closer, Lukas heard heavy, thumping footsteps. The crunch of thick combat boots crushing dry leaves. Whoever controlled that drone was coming. 

"We need to go," Clara whispered, her eyes wide with real fear. "Now."

"Agreed," Devan said quietly. He grabbed Lukas by the shoulder and hauled him up. 

Lukas looked down at the dull grey rock. His heart was pounding. Something inside him told him they shouldn't leave it here for those people to find. If that drone was looking for it, it meant something. 

Without thinking, Lukas reached down and hoisted the stone into his arms. He nearly stumbled—despite losing its glow, the rock was shockingly, impossibly heavy, weighing well over twenty pounds like a solid dumbbell of lead. Grunting against the unnatural density, he shoved it roughly into his empty backpack and zipped it shut. 

"What are you doing?!" Clara whisper-yelled. 

"Taking the evidence," Lukas said firmly. "Run."

The three of them scrambled out of the crater. As soon as their feet hit flat ground, they bolted. Devan took the lead, charting the fastest path through the trees, snapping small branches out of the way so Clara and Lukas could follow. 

Behind them, the drone's hum suddenly grew louder, spinning wildly as its cameras picked up their movement. 

"Target sighted. Three unknowns running South," the robotic voice echoed through the woods. 

"Keep running!" Devan shouted, tossing away any need to be quiet. 

Lukas pumped his legs. Usually, a dead sprint through the woods with a twenty-five-pound stone in his backpack would have left him gasping for air within seconds. But right now, his body felt perfectly in tune. His legs didn't ache. His lungs didn't burn. The energy he had taken from the rock was acting like rocket fuel in his veins. 

They burst out of the tree line and practically crashed into the chain-link fence behind the school field. 

Julian looked up from his keyboard, his mouth dropping open in surprise as Devan effortlessly hopped the fence, grabbed Clara’s hand, and hoisted her over. Lukas tossed his backpack over the top and climbed over right behind her. 

They all hit the grass beside the picnic tables, panting and covered in dirt. 

Almost immediately, the piercing sound of the school bell rang out, signaling the end of the lunch period. Students around the field started walking back toward the building. 

Lukas looked back at the woods. The trees were still. The drone hadn't followed them out of the tree line. 

"What..." Julian stammered, looking at their dirty clothes and wide eyes. "What did you guys find?" 

Lukas pulled his backpack onto his shoulder. It weighed a ton, but it felt like a prize. He looked at Clara and Devan. They were both shaken, trying to act normal as they walked toward the school doors. 

"A rock," Lukas finally answered, his voice barely above a whisper. "We found a rock, Julian. And I think someone very dangerous is looking for it." 

He pressed a hand to his chest. Underneath his shirt, the phantom heartbeat was gone. In its place, something new was awake. And it was hungry for more.
