# Midnight Static
The night sky over Oakhaven was a canvas of undisturbed velvet. Far away from the suffocating neon glare of Meridian City, this quiet suburban town offered something far more valuable to a kid with a head full of dreams: a clear, unobstructed view of the cosmos. 

Lukas sat cross-legged on the slightly sloped asphalt shingles of the orphanage roof, one eye squeezed shut. His other eye was pressed against the eyepiece of a bulky, Frankenstein’s-monster of a telescope. It was held together by gray PVC piping, heavy-duty duct tape, and a set of scavenged optical lenses he had spent three months carefully polishing after rescuing them from the local scrapyard. 

A soldering iron rested safely on a cinderblock nearby, its tip cooling off, while a tangled mess of wires connected a cobbled-together radio receiver to a pair of oversized, battered headphones resting around his neck. 

He tweaked the focus knob. The blurred white smear in the lens snapped into a crisp, glowing cratered sphere. The moon. 

"Still just rock," Lukas murmured to himself, leaning back with a quiet sigh. 

He rubbed his eyes with fingers that were roughened and calloused, scarred by the occasional slip of a wire cutter or the sudden bite of hot solder. At fifteen—or at least, he would officially be fifteen in about ten minutes—Lukas was lanky, all elbows and knees, still waiting for his frame to catch up with his height. 

The air tonight felt different. It was mid-autumn, but the breeze carrying the scent of pine needles from the distant mountain range felt heavy. It wasn't humid, exactly, but the atmosphere felt... *thick*. Every few minutes, a low hum of static would buzz through the headphones around his neck, interrupting the usual white noise of the radio waves he liked to monitor. 

The heavy squeal of rusted hinges broke the silence. 

Lukas didn't jump. He turned his head as the roof access door pushed open. A man stepped out onto the flat landing just below the sloped roof, holding two steaming ceramic mugs. 

"I thought I might find you up here, freezing half to death," a warm, gravely voice said. 

"I've got my jacket, Mr. Mike," Lukas replied, offering a faint, comfortable smile as he shimmied down the shingles to the flat landing. 

Michael—or Mr. Mike, as he was known to the twenty-odd kids living under his roof—was a man who looked like a sturdy, weathered oak tree. He had a thick beard that was steadily losing the battle to gray, deep laugh lines around his eyes, and a habitual gentleness that demanded respect rather than asking for it. 

Mr. Mike handed one of the mugs to Lukas. The sweet, rich scent of instant hot cocoa washed over him. 

"Midnight in three minutes," Mr. Mike noted, glancing at his battered wristwatch, then up at the stars. "Happy Birthday, Lukas."

"Thanks, Mike." Lukas took a sip, letting the warmth chase away the autumn chill settling into his bones. 

Mr. Mike looked at the telescope, then at the tangled radio equipment. "Picking up any alien transmissions on that junk heap tonight?" 

"Just local static," Lukas said, though he tapped the side of his headphones. "Actually, a lot of static tonight. The ionosphere must be acting up. The signals are completely distorted."

Mr. Mike’s smile faltered, just for a fraction of a second. It was a subtle shift—a slight tightening around his eyes as he looked out over the darkened streets of Oakhaven—but Lukas was observant. He spent his life watching how things were put together, and that included people. 

"Is everything okay?" Lukas asked. 

Mr. Mike blinked, the warmth instantly returning to his features. "Of course. Just... reminiscing. Fifteen years. It feels like yesterday I heard you wailing from that alleyway behind the old bakery. It was a night just like this one, actually. The air felt heavy. The weather was strange." He reached out, his large, rough hand squeezing Lukas's shoulder. "You've grown into a fine young man, Lukas. I know you've got your sights set on Meridian University. You're going to build incredible things."

"If I can scrape together the scholarship money," Lukas muttered, looking down at his mug. "And pass advanced calculus."

"You will. Just don't forget to get some sleep occasionally." Mr. Mike gave his shoulder one last squeeze before turning back to the door. "Don't stay up too late. You've got school tomorrow, and you know how Clara gets if you make her late."

Lukas smiled at the mention of his friend. "Goodnight, Mr. Mike."

As the roof door clicked shut, Lukas checked his digital watch. *12:00 AM.* 

Happy birthday. 

As the thought crossed his mind, a sharp, sudden crackle of violent static exploded from his headphones, so loud it made him flinch. At the exact same moment, an intense, localized burst of heat blossomed in the dead center of his chest. It didn't burn like fire; it felt like a tightly coiled wire snapping violently awake. 

Lukas gasped, dropping his mug. It shattered on the concrete, hot cocoa pooling into the dust. He gripped his chest, his breathing ragged. The heat spider-webbed outward, racing down his arms and up his neck like a surge of electrical current beneath his skin. It lasted for a terrifying, breathless three seconds.

And then, it vanished. 

The thick, heavy feeling in the air dissipated, rushing past him like an exhaled breath. Lukas sat on the roof, shivering, his heart hammering against his ribs. He looked at his hands, expecting to see them blistered, but there was nothing. Just his normal, calloused skin. 

He stared at his broken mug, chalking the sudden palpitation up to too much caffeine and not enough sleep.

***

The next morning, the Oakhaven Orphanage was its usual state of controlled anarchy. 

"Who hid the toaster knob? I know it was one of you gremlins!" a voice shouted from the kitchen.

Lukas navigated the narrow hallway, expertly dodging a pair of seven-year-olds racing past with action figures. He entered the kitchen just in time to see Clara interrogating a pair of guilty-looking twins. 

Clara was fifteen, just like him, with unruly brown hair shoved hastily into a messy bun. She was using a butter knife to aggressively point at the twins. One of her hands had a fresh band-aid wrapped around the thumb. 

"They didn't hide it," Lukas said, stepping up and pulling a small, plastic dial from his pocket. "The retention clip snapped. I glued it back together last night. Give it an hour to cure, Clara."

Clara dropped the butter knife with a dramatic sigh, reaching over to steal a slice of dry toast from a nearby plate. "Thank you, Mr. Fix-It. Now I have to eat this cold. Truly, a tragedy."

Lukas laughed, grabbing his battered backpack from a nearby chair. Clara’s domain was anything living; if a kid scraped a knee or caught a cold, or if the garden out back needed reviving, Clara was the genius who fixed it. Her windowsill was a jungle of propagating succulents and weird, hybrid ferns she cross-pollinated just to see what would happen. But when it came to machinery, she had zero patience. 

"Come on," Lukas said, nudging her. "If we miss the bus, we have to walk."

"I don't mind walking," she muttered, grabbing her bag. "Did you know walking barefoot on dirt for ten minutes a day actually reduces inflammation? The Earth has a natural electric charge that neutralizes free radicals in the body."

"If you walk barefoot to Oakhaven High, you'll step on broken glass, not natural electrons," Lukas shot back dryly as they pushed through the front door into the crisp morning air. 

As they walked down the quiet suburban street, lined with maple trees dropping their red and gold leaves onto the sidewalks, they heard the rhythmic *thwap-thwap-thwap* of sneakers on asphalt.

Jogging backward in front of them was Devan. 

"Morning, nerds!" Devan grinned, showing off perfectly straight white teeth. He was of Maluran descent, possessing rich, dark brown skin and a wiry, incredibly athletic frame. Even while jogging backward, he was bouncing lightly on the balls of his feet, doing an elaborate shoulder stretch with his arms behind his head. 

"Devan, if you trip on a crack and crack your skull, I’m not doing first aid," Clara warned.

"I have perfect spatial awareness, Clara," Devan scoffed, spinning around seamlessly on one foot without losing his forward momentum. Devan practiced a traditional martial art his grandfather had brought over from the Malura coast. It involved a dizzying amount of low stances, animalistic movements, and flexibility. Half the time, he couldn't sit normally in a chair. 

"Where’s Julian?" Lukas asked, scanning the sidewalk ahead.

"Oh, the zombie approaches," Devan pointed ahead to the bus stop. 

Slouched against the metal pole of the bus stop sign was Julian. He was buried in a faded, oversized grey hoodie, his pale face illuminated by the bright screen of his handheld console. Thick, noise-canceling headphones covered his ears. Julian looked up, blinking at the sunlight like a creature of the deep ocean exposed to a flashlight.

Lukas walked up and tapped Julian's shoulder. Julian yanked down the headphones, letting a faint barrage of techno-beats bleed into the air. 

"The servers for *Aether-Fall* were atrocious last night," Julian grumbled as a greeting, slipping his console into his pocket. "I was tweaking the packet flow rate for three hours and still had a fifty-millisecond delay. My parries were entirely out of sync." 

"Good morning to you too, Julian," Clara rolled her eyes. 

"Happy birthday, by the way," Julian added, pulling a tangled bundle of cords from his pocket and tossing it to Lukas. "I stripped those copper wires you asked for. Took me forever."

"Thanks, man," Lukas smiled, stuffing the wires into his backpack next to his modified radio. 

The four of them stood in a loose circle as the yellow school bus groaned its way down the street toward them. Beyond the bus, far in the distance across the flat expanse of the plains, the gleaming, needle-like skyscrapers of Meridian City were barely visible through the morning haze. 

"I heard the acceptance rate for MCU dropped to four percent this year," Clara mentioned quietly, her eyes tracking the distant skyline. Her voice lacked its usual sarcasm, replaced by a quiet longing.

Devan stopped stretching for a moment, standing tall and following her gaze. "Four percent doesn't matter. We're going. I'm going to get a sports scholarship for kinesiology, Julian is going to hack their servers and enroll himself, you’re going to cure some disease with a fern, and Lukas is going to build a robot that writes their entrance exams."

"That's not how robotics works," Lukas chuckled, but he felt that familiar warmth in his chest. Meridian Central University wasn't just a school to them; it was the finish line of their childhoods. A ticket out of the mundane quiet of Oakhaven. 

"Besides," Julian muttered, pushing his glasses up his nose as the bus doors squeaked open. "I don't need to hack them. My GPA is higher than all of yours." 

As they boarded the bus, taking their usual seats in the back, Lukas slung his backpack off his shoulders. When it bumped against his knee, the modified radio inside let out a bizarre, high-pitched *whine*—like the sound of a microphone being brought too close to a speaker. 

At the exact same time, Lukas felt a prickle beneath his skin. It started at his fingertips, a strange, bubbling sensation, before creeping up his forearm. It wasn't painful, but it felt remarkably like the heat he had experienced on the roof at midnight. 

He looked down at his hand. 

For half a second, just the fraction of a heartbeat, he could have sworn he saw thin, glowing blue veins tracing themselves just beneath the surface of his skin, illuminating his callouses from the inside out. 

He blinked hard. The bus bounced over a pothole. The sensation vanished. His hand was completely normal.

"You good, man?" Devan asked, looking over from the adjacent seat. "You look like you've seen a ghost."

"Yeah," Lukas swallowed hard, flexing his fingers. They felt stronger, somehow. Lighter. "Yeah, I'm fine. Must just be the static electricity in here."

He looked out the smudged window of the bus as Oakhaven rolled by. The town looked exactly the same as it had yesterday, completely unchanged, blissfully mundane. But as Lukas pressed a hand to his chest, feeling a faint, unfamiliar hum beneath his ribs, he couldn't shake the feeling that his world was never going to be the same again.
