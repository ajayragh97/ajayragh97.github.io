# Chapter 4: The Contraband

Walking back into the school building was the hardest part. 

Lukas’s heart was hammering against his ribs, sounding uncomfortably loud in his ears. Just ninety seconds ago, they had been running for their lives from a high-tech black drone in the woods. Now, they were standing in a hallway that smelled of cheap pine cleaner, surrounded by kids talking about math homework and weekend plans. 

The contrast made his brain hurt. 

"Act normal," Devan muttered under his breath, vigorously dusting dry leaves off his pants. "Don't look guilty. Guilty people walk fast. Walk slow."

Clara frantically tried to comb the twigs out of her messy bun using her fingers. "I can't believe we just did that. My shoes are ruined. And if Mr. Mike finds out we were trespassing in the woods..."

"Mr. Mike is the least of our worries right now," Lukas whispered back. His backpack strap was digging painfully into his shoulder. The dull grey rock he had taken weighed at least fifteen pounds, making his bag slump heavily. 

Julian, who had reluctantly packed up his mechanical keyboard and followed them inside, was practically vibrating with nervous curiosity. 

"You guys look insane," Julian hissed, adjusting his glasses. "What did you actually find? Why were you running? Was it a bear? Please tell me it wasn't a bear, those things can climb chain-link fences."

"Not a bear," Devan said, keeping his eyes forward as they merged with the crowd of students heading to their next class. "More like a drone. A military-grade drone. And ground forces."

Julian stopped walking. A student behind him bumped into his shoulder, grumbling, but Julian didn't notice. "Wait. What?"

"We’ll talk about it after school," Lukas said firmly, grabbing the back of Julian's oversized hoodie and dragging him forward so they wouldn't block traffic. "In my shed at the orphanage. Not here."

The warning bell blared through the speakers overhead. The group had to split up. Devan had gym, Julian and Clara had English, and Lukas had World History. 

"Meet by the front gates right after the final bell," Clara whispered to the boys. She gave Lukas a pointed look, her eyes darting to his heavy backpack. "Don't let anyone see what's in there, Lukas."

Lukas nodded. He tightened the straps on his bag, turned, and walked into his History class. 

He slid into his seat in the middle row just as Mr. Harrison, an older teacher who always spoke in a boring, flat drone, started writing on the chalkboard. Lukas gently lowered his backpack to the floor between his feet. It hit the linoleum with a solid, heavy *thud*.

The girl sitting next to him glanced over, raising an eyebrow, but quickly looked back at her notebook. It was Elena, the transfer student from his Physics class. Of course she was in this class too. 

Lukas kept his eyes on the front of the room, but he wasn't paying a single bit of attention to the lecture on the Industrial Revolution. His mind was racing a mile a minute. 

Usually, sitting in a warm classroom right after lunch made him feel sluggish and sleepy. But today, his eyes were wide open. The mysterious exhaustion he had felt all morning was completely gone. The energy he had pulled from the glowing blue rock was still sitting inside him. It felt like a battery strapped to his ribs, fully charged, humming with an icy, refreshing power. 

Under the desk, he rubbed his thumb against his palm. When he closed his eyes, he could almost trace the path of the energy flowing through his arms and chest. It wasn't electric, and it wasn't heat. It just felt like pure *fuel*. 

*What did I absorb?* Lukas thought, his eyebrows pulled tight. *And why do I feel like I've known how to hold this stuff my whole life?*

His thoughts were interrupted by his phone buzzing gently in his pocket. Keeping his head down, he carefully slid it out beneath the desk. It was a text message from a group chat titled *'The Junk Yard'*.

**Clara (1:12 PM):** *I hope you put a padlock on that backpack.* 

**Devan (1:13 PM):** *gym dodgeball is boring today. i feel way too pumped. i could flip a car rn.* 

**Julian (1:13 PM):** *I am severely out of the loop and feeling very marginalized.* 

Lukas smiled faintly and typed a quick, one-handed response. 

**Lukas (1:14 PM):** *Backpack is secure. We open it at the shed at 4 PM.* 

He hit send, but just as he slid his phone back into his pocket, his phone buzzed one more time. He pulled it back out.

**Clara (1:14 PM):** *Don't freak out. But I took something too.*

Lukas stared at the screen. He quickly texted back. 

**Lukas (1:15 PM):** *What do you mean you took something?!*

**Clara (1:15 PM):** *The purple plants around the crater. They were doing something impossible. I snapped a root off right before the drone showed up. It's in my pencil case. See you at 4.*

Lukas let out a quiet groan and put his head in his hands. He thought *he* was crazy for picking up a strange alien rock, but at least his rock looked like a normal piece of concrete now. Clara had grabbed a piece of glowing, moving vegetation. If the people with the drone were tracking what fell in the woods, they were both walking targets now.

Suddenly, a strange feeling washed over him. It wasn't the energy inside his chest; it was a sudden prickle on the back of his neck, like the feeling you get when you know someone is watching you. 

Lukas sat up straight and looked toward the large row of windows on the left side of the classroom. The windows looked out over the front parking lot of the school and the main road. 

Moving slowly down the street, almost crawling against the curb, was a large, windowless black utility van. There were no logos or phone numbers printed on the side. The tinted windows were pitch black. 

The van rolled to a complete stop right next to the school's front gate. It sat there, its engine quietly idling. 

Lukas's heart skipped a beat. 

They didn't see our faces, Lukas reminded himself. *We had our backs to the drone. They just saw three kids running out of the woods. There are eight hundred kids in this school.*

Even so, he couldn't tear his eyes away from the black vehicle. For three agonizing minutes, it just parked there. Finally, a small amber light flashed, and the van slowly rolled away, disappearing around the corner. 

Lukas exhaled a breath he didn't realize he had been holding. 

"Fascinating, isn't it?" a quiet voice murmured from his right. 

Lukas jumped, snapping his head over. Elena was looking at him. Her dark eyes darted from the window, down to his heavily slumped backpack on the floor, and then finally met his gaze. She hadn't been taking notes for the last five minutes. She had been watching him. 

"What's fascinating?" Lukas asked, trying to sound as casual as possible. 
 
"Your sudden spike in adrenaline," Elena said softly. Her tone wasn't mean, just brutally observant. "Your pupils are dilated. Your breathing rate doubled when that van parked. You were perfectly calm before." She tilted her head slightly. "Is there something interesting in your bag, Lukas?"

"Just textbooks," Lukas lied smoothly. "They're really heavy. Hurts my back."
 
Elena stared at him for a long, uncomfortable moment. She clearly didn't believe a word he said. "Of course. Textbooks." 

She unzipped the meticulously organized front pocket of her binder and slid out a device that looked like a bulky graphing calculator with a small antenna attached to it. The screen showed a perfectly mapped mathematical sine wave. Except, right now, the wave was being completely corrupted by violent, rhythmic spikes.

"I monitor low-frequency atmospheric noise during the day. Usually to track solar flares," Elena whispered, tapping her pen against the glass screen. "But for the last three minutes, there has been a massive, localized electromagnetic sweep pulsing from the street. The wave frequency is unnatural. It’s an active spatial sweep, like high-yield ground-penetrating radar. They aren't parking. They are mapping the density of everything inside this building."
 
Lukas froze. He looked at the jagged graph on her screen, then up at her dark, intense eyes. 

She slid the device back into her binder, zipping it shut with quiet precision. "Whatever is in your bag, Lukas... it must have a massive density profile to warrant that kind of equipment. I highly suggest you don't drop it."

Lukas swallowed hard and faced the chalkboard. The rest of the afternoon dragged on endlessly. Every ticking second of the classroom clock felt like an hour. Lukas was painfully aware of his own body—the energy humming in his veins, the dead weight of the rock at his feet, and the sharp, calculating girl sitting next to him. 

The mundane, normal world of Oakhaven High felt completely different now. The walls had thinned. The rules had changed. And whatever came crashing into the woods today was only the beginning.
