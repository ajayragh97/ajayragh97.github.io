#  Anomalous Variables

Oakhaven High was a monument to mediocrity, built in the late seventies and suffering from a permanent smell of floor wax and stale tater tots. The fluorescent lights overhead always buzzed at a frequency that gave Julian a migraine, and the heating system rattled like a dying asthmatic. 

To Lukas, though, it was just the daily grind—another set of hours to get through before he could get back to his workbench. 

"I'm telling you, the drop rates are rigged," Julian was complaining, leaning against a dented blue locker as Lukas spun the combination dial on his own. "A zero-point-two percent chance for the Aether-Core drop? It mathematically forces a pay-to-win model. It’s predatory."

"Or maybe," Devan said, stretching his hamstrings by propping his foot onto a bench completely parallel to his waist, "you just suck at the raid boss. You gotta dodge, man. Move your fingers faster."

Julian gave him a withering look from beneath his grey hood. "It’s a localized network issue, Devan. My APM is flawless. My ping, however, is a tragedy."

Lukas grabbed his physics textbook and slammed the locker shut, but the metal door caught on the latch. He let out a breath and slammed his hand against it again to force it closed. 

*Zzt.* 

Lukas yanked his hand back with a sharp hiss. A visible arc of blue static had jumped from the metal locker to the center of his palm. His fingers instantly went numb, followed by a fierce, tingling ache. 

"Whoa, nasty static shock," Devan noted, finally putting his leg down. "You shuffling your feet on the carpet, sparky?"

Lukas didn't answer immediately. He stared at his palm. The center of it was faintly red, but the numbness was already fading, replaced by that same bizarre, bubbling warmth he had felt on the bus, and on the roof the night before. It felt like carbonated water rushing through his veins. 

"Yeah," Lukas mumbled, closing his hand into a tight fist to suppress a tremor he couldn't quite control. "Just static. The air is really dry today."

"Well, hurry up, or we’ll be late for Harris," Julian said, already shuffling down the linoleum hallway toward the science wing. 

Advanced Physics with Mr. Harris was usually Lukas’s favorite class. It was a tangible, predictable world. Objects in motion stayed in motion. Energy was conserved. For a kid who grew up not knowing where he came from, the iron-clad rules of the universe were immensely comforting. Math didn't lie. 

Today, they were doing practical circuit labs. The classroom was filled with rows of black-topped lab tables, each equipped with breadboards, copper wiring, small led lights, and power supply boxes. 

Lukas took his usual seat near the back. Julian sat to his left, already pulling out his laptop to 'take notes'—which actually meant coding a script to automate his video game grinding. 

To Lukas’s right was an empty stool. At least, it usually was. 

As the bell gave a sickly, dying ring, a girl slid into the seat, dropping a meticulously organized binder onto the black slate. Lukas blinked, pulling his attention away from the tingling in his palm. 

It was the new transfer student. Elena. 

She had dark hair cut into a sharp, precise bob and wore a crisp white sweater that made her look like she belonged in the pristine, climate-controlled corridors of a Meridian University library, rather than the dusty lab of Oakhaven High. She had been here for two weeks, and Lukas didn't think he had heard her say a single word outside of answering Mr. Harris's questions with terrifying, machine-like accuracy. 

She didn't look at Lukas, her dark, intense eyes already fixed on the whiteboard as Mr. Harris began writing out Ohm's Law. 

"Alright, people, settle down," Mr. Harris said, wiping chalk dust on his worn corduroy pants. "Today we are looking at electrical resistance. I want you to pair up. Build the circuit diagram on page forty-two. Measure the voltage drop across the three resistors. If your LED explodes, you get a zero for the day. Begin."

Julian didn't even look up from his screen. "I’m legally blind today, Lukas. You build it, I’ll copy the data."

Lukas sighed, accustomed to this arrangement. He actually liked doing the physical work anyway. He pulled the plastic tackle box of components toward him and started slotting the small, color-banded resistors into the breadboard. 

As his fingers brushed the copper wire, the hum in his chest flared up. 

It was distracting. It felt like he had drank four cans of energy drinks on an empty stomach. His heart was beating at a normal pace, but there was a secondary, phantom pulse thumping somewhere deep beneath his sternum. 

He picked up the red and black leads connecting to the small power supply on the desk. It was turned off, dialed down to zero volts. 

Lukas connected the black ground wire to the board. Then, holding the exposed metal clip of the red positive wire between his thumb and forefinger, he moved to attach it to the main rail. 

He felt a sudden, sharp tightness in his chest. Like a dam groaning under pressure. 

A jolt of heat shot down his right arm, bypassing his muscles and riding entirely along some unseen inner highway. It pooled in his fingertips right as he pinched the metal clip. 

A sharp *pop* echoed at the table. 

Lukas gasped, dropping the wire. 

The small red LED bulb plugged into the breadboard had flashed brilliantly, blindingly white for a fraction of a second, before bursting with a loud crack, showering the black tabletop with fragments of plastic and glass. A thin wisp of acrid grey smoke curled up from the ruined diode.

"Whoa!" Julian flinched, slamming his laptop shut defensively. "Dude! What did you do?" 

Lukas sat frozen, his eyes wide. He looked from the shattered bulb to his hand. 

The power supply box was still switched *off*. The dial was still set to zero. There was no electricity flowing into that board. None. 

"Mr. Vance," Mr. Harris sighed from the front of the room, looking over his spectacles at the smoke. "I specifically said no exploding LEDs."

"I... I didn't," Lukas stammered, his mind racing, trying to desperately calculate the physics of what just happened. Had a capacitor retained a charge? No, they weren't using capacitors. Was the power supply faulty and leaking current? He checked the switch. Completely disengaged. 

"The power unit isn't on," a quiet, analytical voice spoke up from his right. 

Lukas looked over. Elena was leaning slightly across the aisle, her sharp eyes studying the burnt scorch mark on his breadboard. She held a digital multimeter in her hand. Without asking, she reached over and touched the two probes of her meter to the power leads of Lukas's box. 

"Zero point zero volts," Elena muttered, her brow furrowing in genuine confusion. She looked up at Lukas, her eyes narrowing. "How did you overload a diode with zero voltage?"

"I... static?" Lukas offered, rubbing his thumb against his palm. The heat was receding, draining back into his chest, leaving him feeling bizarrely fatigued, as if he had just sprinted a hundred-meter dash. 

Elena stared at him like he was a math equation that wasn't balancing. "A human body can carry a static charge of a few thousand volts, yes, but the amperage is nearly nonexistent. To blow a diode like that, with that much heat, you would need sustained current. Not static." 

"Maybe I rubbed my socks really hard on the carpet," Lukas deflected, giving a weak, uncomfortable laugh. 

Elena didn't laugh. She just looked at his hand, then back at his face, committing the anomaly to memory before silently sliding back to her own table and opening her textbook. 

"Minus ten points for the hardware casualty, Lukas," Mr. Harris called out. "Grab another diode from the back and actually turn your power source on this time."

"Right. Sorry." 

Lukas spent the rest of the hour using a pair of insulated tweezers to handle the components. He couldn't shake the exhaustion clinging to his bones, nor the metallic taste that had suddenly bloomed on the back of his tongue. 

Something was wrong with him. Medical, maybe? Neurological? 

Could a nerve condition cause localized sweating or electrical discharge? He made a mental note to ask Clara later. She read medical journals for fun. 

***

At lunch, the crisp autumn air provided some relief from the stuffy corridors. The four of them claimed their usual territory: a cluster of dilapidated wooden picnic tables near the edge of the school’s athletic field, backed up against a chain-link fence separating the school from a dense strip of pine forest. 

Clara was eating an apple with a pocket knife, cutting remarkably precise, paper-thin slices. Devan was doing pull-ups on the sturdy overhanging branch of an oak tree above them, while Julian aggressively typed away on a mechanical keyboard he had inexplicably brought outside. 

Lukas sat heavily at the table, dropping his bag and massaging his chest. 

"You look terrible," Clara noted without looking up, handing him an apple slice on the blade of her knife. "You have dark circles under your eyes. Worse than usual. Vitamin iron deficiency? Did Mr. Mike run out of spinach again?"

"I'm fine," Lukas took the slice. "Just didn't sleep well. Had a weird physics class."

Clara finally looked at him, her practical gaze instantly softening into something motherly and diagnostic. She reached across the table and pressed the back of her hand against his forehead. "You're burning up."

"I am?" Lukas frowned. He felt exhausted, yes, but not feverish. 

"Slightly," Clara amended, pulling her hand back. "You feel... dry. Drink water. And take this." She dug into her canvas bag, produced a small glass vial filled with a weird, amber-colored liquid, and pushed it across the table. 

Lukas stared at it. "What is that?"

"Pine needle extract, a bit of honey, and some crushed willow bark," Clara said proudly. "Natural salicylic acid. Reduces fevers and aches. Made it myself."

"I am not drinking your swamp juice, Clara. Last time you gave Devan a tincture, he spent an hour in the bathroom."

"It was an *herbal cleanse*," Devan corrected between pull-ups. "And honestly, I felt remarkably lighter afterward. My high kicks gained at least two inches of clearance."

Lukas pushed the vial back. "I'll stick to ibuprofen, thanks." 

He leaned his forearms on the table, looking past the chain-link fence and into the dense woods behind the school. The trees were swaying slightly, though Lukas didn't feel much of a breeze. 

"Hey," Lukas started, hesitant. "Hypothetically... if someone experienced sudden, massive localized heat and static discharge... what would cause that?" 

Clara paused her slicing. "Environmental or biological?"

"Biological."

"Neuropathy," Clara answered immediately. "Nerve damage causing phantom burning sensations. Or an electrolyte severe imbalance causing muscular misfiring. Why? Who are we diagnosing?"

Lukas was about to brush it off, to say he read it in an article, but he didn't get the chance. 

*Thrum.*

The sound was low, barely audible, more of a vibration that rattled through the wood of the picnic table than a sound you could hear with your ears. 

Devan dropped from the branch, landing silently on the grass. He frowned, his head tilting toward the woods. "Did you guys feel that?" 

"Feel what?" Julian asked, keeping his eyes on his screen. "An earthquake? I'm pretty sure we don't live on a fault line."

"No. Not an earthquake." Devan walked to the chain link fence, his fingers hooking into the metal diamonds. His usually jovial face was completely flat, hyper-focused. "Something heavy hit the ground. Out there."

Lukas felt the metallic tang return to his mouth. The phantom pulse beneath his sternum beat once, hard. 

"Probably just a dead tree falling," Clara said logically, putting her knife away. "The woods are full of rot this time of year." 

"A tree falling creates an acoustic echo," Devan said, his eyes scanning the shadowed treeline. "That didn't echo. It was just a thud." 

Lukas stood up. The fatigue was temporarily pushed away by an unnatural, tugging sensation in his gut, pulling him exactly toward the spot Devan was looking at. 

He didn't know it, but the microscopic fragments of ambient mana settling in his newly awakened pathways were starving. They were on Pangea, an energy desert. And like a desperate, dying man smelling a drop of water in the Sahara, Lukas’s body was reacting to something that had just forcefully deposited itself a quarter of a mile into the Oakhaven woods.

"I'm going to take a look," Lukas found himself saying. 

"We only have twenty minutes of lunch left," Julian pointed out, annoyed at the interruption to his coding. 

"We won't go far," Devan grinned, his sense of adventure overriding his caution. He effortlessly vaulted the six-foot chain-link fence, landing gracefully in a bed of dead leaves. "Come on, birthday boy. Let's see what a falling tree looks like."

Lukas looked at Clara. She sighed dramatically, packing her things into her bag. "If you two get poison ivy, I charge ten bucks a bottle for the calamine lotion."

Lukas climbed the fence, far less gracefully than his friend, dropping onto the forest floor. As his boots hit the dirt, the humming in his chest grew louder, vibrating against his ribs. 

Whatever had fallen out there... he somehow knew, with an irrational, absolute certainty, that it wasn't a tree.
