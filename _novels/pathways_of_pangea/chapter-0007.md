# Chapter 7: Chemical Evacuation

The school’s linoleum hallways blurred as Lukas sprinted. The blaring klaxon of the fire alarm echoed off the metal lockers, making the entire building vibrate. 

Teachers were standing in their doorways, ushering confused students out toward the football field for a standard fire evacuation. Lukas ignored their shouts. The energy surging in his chest had completely taken over. It flooded his leg muscles, propelling him forward with a speed that felt entirely unnatural. 

He forced himself to take a deep, shuddering breath, trying to dial back the raw power flowing through his arms. *Add resistance,* he reminded himself frantically. He shoved his glowing hands deep into the front pockets of his jeans just as he drifted around the corner into the science wing. 

The biology classroom wasn't a high-tech facility. It was just an outdated, underfunded lab with worn black resin tables, wobbly wooden stools, and safety showers that probably hadn't been tested in a decade. Right now, its heavy wooden door was propped wide open.

Students from the sixth-period class were already shuffling out the emergency exit at the end of the hall, whispering and trying to look back. 

Lukas pushed past the last few stragglers, reached the doorway, and froze. 

The lab was a mess. Half the plastic stools were overturned. In the center of the room, standing completely still with her hands raised defensively, was Clara. 

Standing a few feet away from her was Mr. Avery, the biology teacher. He was clutching a standard heavy red fire extinguisher, holding the hose aimed directly at Clara’s lab table. 

"Step back from the table, Clara! The fire alarm is pulled, you need to evacuate!" Mr. Avery yelled, his voice cracking with stress. "It could be off-gassing toxic fumes!"

"It doesn't have fumes, Mr. Avery, please just give me a glass beaker to put it in!" Clara pleaded, her voice trembling but surprisingly grounded. 

Lukas looked past them to Clara’s desk. 

Her stainless steel thermos was completely ruined. But it hadn’t exploded; it had collapsed inward, like it had been crushed and dissolved by a powerful acid. Pushing its way out of the destroyed, sludgy metal was the root. 

Except, it wasn't just a root anymore. Over the last few hours, gorging itself on Clara’s scissors and the thermos lining, it had grown. It was now the size of a thick grapevine, roughly three feet long, pulsing with a sickly, purple glow. Tiny, needle-like tendrils were thrashing the air, trying to anchor themselves. 

Worse, it was creeping across the table directly toward the metal nozzle of the gas line. If that thing started dissolving the school's gas pipes to eat the iron, they would have a very real, explosive leak on their hands. 

"I'm spraying it!" Mr. Avery shouted, stepping forward to pull the pin on the extinguisher. 

"No, the chemical foam will ruin the cell structure!" Clara yelled, stepping in front of the table. 

"Lukas," a voice whispered sharply behind him. 

Lukas jumped. Elena had slipped into the doorway right behind him, defying the evacuation orders. She stood at his shoulder, her dark eyes wide as she took in the impossible, moving purple vine on the table. 

"That's not native flora," Elena whispered, stepping into the room. "The locomotive speed alone... it's aggressively hunting for minerals." 

"Stay here," Lukas ordered. 

He stepped into the classroom just as the purple vine finally reached the metal gas nozzle. The plant’s tendrils immediately wrapped around the cold iron, preparing to metabolize the pipe. 

Lukas didn't think. He let instinct and the roaring hum in his chest take over. 

"Lukas, what are you doing?!" Mr. Avery shouted. "Get out of here!"

Lukas ignored the teacher, stepping up to the lab table. He pulled his right hand out of his pocket. The neon-blue lines beneath his skin flared faintly. He reached out and grabbed the thick purple vine with his bare hand, attempting to rip it off the nozzle.

The second his skin made contact with the alien plant, a violent reaction occurred. 

The ambient, icy energy in Lukas’s body violently clashed with the bizarre, biological power of the root. A loud, sharp *snap* echoed in the classroom, like the sound of a heavy static shock. 

A bright blue spark flashed between Lukas's palm and the purple bark. 

The plant shrieked. It wasn't a vocal sound, but a high-frequency vibration that rattled the glass beakers on the nearby shelves. The plant immediately released the iron pipe, recoiling from Lukas's touch as if it had been burned by fire. Its purple glow dimmed dramatically, retreating into itself until it looked like a shriveled, dead, grey root. 

Lukas stood there, his chest heaving, his hand tingling violently from the clash. The vine lay perfectly still on the black resin table, completely stunned by the energy Lukas had just discharged. 

"Glass jar. Now!" Lukas snapped, looking at Clara. 

Clara snapped out of her shock. She dove toward the storage cupboards at the back of the room, grabbing a thick glass jar usually used for storing formaldehyde specimens. She ran back, slammed it upside down over the shriveled root, flipped it over, and screwed the heavy plastic lid on tight. 

Silence descended on the classroom, broken only by the wailing of the fire alarm overhead. 

Mr. Avery slowly lowered the heavy fire extinguisher. He stared at the jar in Clara’s hands, then looked at the ruined puddle of metal that used to be a thermos. Finally, he looked at Lukas, completely bewildered. 

"What... what just happened?" Mr. Avery breathed, wiping sweat from his forehead. 

Lukas felt his heart rate spiking. How was he supposed to explain away a glowing vine eating a thermos? 

"A prank," a cool, steady voice cut in. 

Elena stepped up to the table. She completely ignored the destroyed thermos and focused directly on Mr. Avery, her face a mask of polite annoyance. 

"A prank?" Mr. Avery repeated. 

"A very stupid one," Elena lied smoothly, not missing a beat. "Clara was trying to simulate a highly reactive expanding foam using industrial solvent and dyed polymers. She hid it in the thermos so she wouldn't get caught. The chemical reaction was exothermic; it accelerated, melted the seal, and breached the metal." 

Mr. Avery stared at her, then looked back at the jar. Inside, the root was completely still, looking exactly like a piece of melted, grey plastic. 

"It was moving," Mr. Avery said slowly, pointing a shaking finger at it. 

"Thermal expansion and escaping gas pockets," Elena stated without blinking, throwing out standard textbook physics. "It twists as the heat leaves it. Lukas touched it, which disturbed the thermal structure and caused a static pop. It's just a toxic chemical toy, Mr. Avery. Nothing biological."

Mr. Avery was a high school biology teacher, not an organic chemist. Confronted with a calm, rational explanation from the smartest student in the junior class, his brain actively chose to accept it over the impossibility of an alien plant. 

"Chemicals," Mr. Avery breathed, rubbing his temples. A flush of pure anger rapidly replaced his fear. "Give me the jar, Clara." 

"Mr. Avery, I can just throw it in the hazardous waste bin—" Clara started, her knuckles turning white around the glass. 

"Give me the jar right now, or I am having you expelled," Mr. Avery snapped, holding his hand out. "I pulled the fire alarm for this. There is a fire engine pulling into the front parking lot as we speak. I have to take this out there and let the fire chief inspect it so we can give the all-clear for toxic fumes." 

With shaking hands, Clara handed the glass jar over. Mr. Avery gripped it tightly, gave all three of them a furious look, and marched out of the classroom to meet the first responders out front. 

Lukas felt a cold pit drop into his stomach as he watched the teacher leave. 

The entire school being evacuated didn't matter. The detention they were going to get didn't matter. 

What mattered was that the local fire department was about to log an incident report about a highly corrosive, anomalous material melting solid steel inside a high school. And anyone actively monitoring the emergency radio channels would hear exactly what happened. 

"Thermal expansion doesn't generate localized electromagnetic pulses," Elena whispered softly. 

Lukas looked over. She was staring at his hand. 

"I don't know what's in that jar, Lukas," she continued, her voice incredibly quiet but razor-sharp. "But I know what I saw under your skin. You didn't disturb its structure... you shocked it." 

Lukas pulled his hand away, shoving it deep into his pocket. His silence was all the answer she needed. 

Outside the window, they could hear the heavy rumble of the local fire engine idling near the front doors. They had lost the plant. It was in the hands of the authorities now. 

And out there, parked somewhere on the suburban streets of Oakhaven, the people looking for the multiversal debris had just found exactly where it was hiding.
